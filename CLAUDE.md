## OM extraction ruleset (when Eric says "extract for KPR" or drops an OM PDF)
- **HARD INVARIANT — an OM must NEVER be routed/treated as a leasing flyer (Eric, 7/2/26).** The upload router classifies each PDF (`docClassify.ts` → `classifyDocument`/`applyDeterministicOverrides`, consumed in `UploadQueue.tsx`). A full JLL OM once got sent down the fast "flyer" path because its exec-summary markets vacancy lease-up ("Currently Available **for Lease** Up") and the old `detectFlyer` matched the bare phrase "for lease." FIVE layered safeguards now prevent it, do NOT weaken them: (1) `detectFlyer` filename guard — a file named `…Offering Memorandum…`/`…OM…` never promotes to flyer; (2) `detectFlyer` sale-signal guard via `hasOmSaleSignals` (offering memorandum/summary, investment sales, NOI, cap rate, asking price, as-is NOI, sale comps…) — present ⇒ never a flyer; (3) `applyDeterministicOverrides` DOWNGRADE — even a direct LLM "flyer" flips to "om" when sale signals exist; (4) length guard — a doc ≥8 pages is never a flyer; (5) a final routing gate in `UploadQueue` that refuses to act on "flyer" when sale signals or length say otherwise. `hasOmSaleSignals` is the single source of truth and is intentionally one-directional (guards OM→flyer, not the reverse — so it excludes flyer-ambiguous tokens like "$/SF"). Tests: `docClassify.test.ts`.
- Output format: **flat deal fields wrapped in `{"deals": [{...all fields...}]}`** — matches the app's "Upload OMs → Upload JSON" button. NOT the nested `{id, data}` backup format. Deliver as a downloadable `.json`.
- **Always include the full deal** in the JSON (never a partial patch) — the bulk importer merges by address/propertyName match and overwrites non-preserved fields.
- **Tenants:** lease occupants only (rent roll / lease schedule with SF and/or rent at THIS address). Never competitors, comp-set, shadow-anchor-at-other-parcel, or trade-area mentions. All into the structured `tenants` array, not summary text.
- **Dates:** ISO `YYYY-MM-DD` everywhere (leaseStart, leaseExpiry, originalLeaseDate) — never `Mon-YYYY` strings.
- `rentSchedule` (**required for every tenant, never null**): all **future** dated rent steps as of the most recent OM/rent-roll date (drop past steps), including option-period bumps; note "flat" if flat.
- `annualRent` = **base only.** Recoveries go in SEPARATE numeric annual-$ fields **only when the OM/roll discloses them**: `expenseReimbursements` (CAM+tax+insurance), `percentageRent`, `otherRent`. Undisclosed → null.
- **Occupancy cost:** NEVER base÷sales, NEVER guess. `occupancyCost %` = (base+reimbursements+pctRent+other) ÷ gross sales, only when components are disclosed; else null. If the OM states an occ-cost %, use it.
- **WALT:** always calculate from lease expiry dates weighted by SF if not stated; never leave null when dates exist.
- `notes`: 5–8 sentence institutional underwriting narrative (asset/location, anchor quality+sales, inline mix, key metrics vs market, thesis, top risk) — references real OM numbers. Never a 2–3 sentence summary.
- `parentCompany`: only when confident (major public corps — TJX, Gap Inc, Ahold Delhaize, etc.). For regional/private/PE-owned, **ask Eric to confirm** before writing it. Same for tenant merges.
- Sales formatting: ≥$1M → "$2.0M / $157 PSF"; <$1M → "$930k".

## Owned-asset extraction — THE RENT ROLL IS THE FINAL SAY (Eric's standing instruction, 6/22/26)
When building/refreshing a deal from an owned-asset document set (rent roll + leasing flyer + sales report + lease abstract), the **current rent roll is authoritative and accurate — it is the most recent record and it WINS every roster conflict.** For every roster fact — tenant name, suite, SF, current rent, commencement/expiry dates, rent steps, options — take the rent roll as final.
- The flyer, sales report, and abstract are **disambiguation aids and cross-checks only.** Use them to untangle column-jumbled MRI rent-roll PDF text (the roll's per-tenant text often interleaves; reconstruct by tying out to the roll's printed occupied-SF and base-rent TOTALS), or to fill clause detail the roll doesn't carry (co-tenancy, exclusives, terminations). **Never use them to override the roll or to cast doubt on a roll figure.** Marketing flyers in particular carry stale/swapped tenant↔box labels — the roll corrects them, not the other way around.
- A figure that comes FROM the roll is settled — do NOT flag it as "verify the footprint/SF against the lease." Flag only what is genuinely NOT on the roll (e.g. co-tenancy/exclusive clauses sourced from a dated abstract) or genuinely absent (NOI/opex when there's no operating statement). Tie-out to the roll's own totals (occupied SF, base rent, gross rent) is the proof the roster is right.
- This is scoped to DEAL/ROSTER EXTRACTION for the library. It does NOT change the lease-ABSTRACTING rules below, where executed legal documents govern and the roll is only a tripwire.

### Owned-asset financials & roster reconciliation — HARD-LEARNED NUANCES (6/22/26, learned across Bensalem/Blue Bell/Lawndale/Deer Creek/Academy)
1. **NOI from KPR's owner income statements: STRIP non-property treasury income.** The MRI "Comparative Income Statement" (Financial Format `dtx_ibs`, "As Of Dec 20XX … (entity) … Basis: Cash/Accrual") books **Interest Income (acct 57100000), Dividend Income (58600000), and MMF Gain/Loss (58400000)** into "TOTAL OTHER INCOME" **above** the NET OPERATING INCOME line — so the statement's *reported* NOI is INFLATED by entity treasury income. Compute **property NOI = (Total Rental Income + Total Expense Reimbursement + property-tenant other income [late fees, lease-termination fees, write-offs]) − Total Operating Expenses**, EXCLUDING interest/dividends/MMF. Set `effectiveGrossIncome` to that clean property EGI so **EGI − OpEx = NOI ties** (and passes the NOI=EGI−OpEx audit). In `keyAssumptions`, state BOTH the clean property NOI and the statement's reported NOI for reconciliation, naming the $ stripped. Inflation seen: Blue Bell $54,600, Bensalem $76,572, Deer Creek $50,720 (mostly a $48,199 dividend); Academy & Lawndale had NO treasury income (already clean — don't strip what isn't there). Late/termination fees and write-offs ARE property income — keep them in EGI. Debt service, depreciation, amortization, capex, leasing costs are all BELOW NOI — never in OpEx.
2. **The roster MUST include VACANT suites so it reconciles to the roll's stated occupancy/GLA.** Build to the roll's printed **total GLA**, not just occupied SF: add a `Vacant` row (`"name":"Vacant"`, `"leaseType":"Vacant"`, `annualRent:0`, `rentPerSF:0`) for every vacant suite the roll lists, so **Σ tenant SF = totalSF** and occupied/total = the stated occupancy. An occupied-only roster silently under-builds the asset and trips the roster-SF-vs-GLA audit. (Blue Bell missed 4,489 SF across 2 suites; Deer Creek missed 22,267 SF incl. the former Buy Buy Baby box — both caught by the tie-out, not by eye.)
3. **NEVER fabricate a suite number.** If the roll excerpt doesn't give a vacant unit's suite, OMIT the `suite` field and note "suite # not captured" — don't invent one (same discipline as never fabricating a figure).
4. **Debt: derive/confirm the loan from the income statement's actual interest line.** Monthly 1st-mortgage interest × 12 = annual debt service; ÷ the rate = implied loan (tie to the statement to the dollar). When Eric gives the rate (e.g. Blue Bell "3.634% fixed all-in"), USE it and verify it reproduces the statement's monthly interest ($19.46M × 3.634% = $707,176 = $58,931/mo ✓). When loan amount/rate/maturity aren't in the folder, record `debtNotes` with the interest figure + "request the loan documents" and leave the rest null — never fabricate amount/rate.
5. **0-SF income-producing leases (ATM / ground / pad / billboard) are NOT NAP.** They pay rent that IS in the roll's base-rent total, so include them as ordinary 0-SF tenants (so base ties to the roll). Marking them `isNAP:true` drops their rent from the roll-up and breaks the GPR/base tie-out. Reserve NAP for genuinely not-owned parcels. (Lawndale's Wells Fargo ATM = $14,520/yr, 0 SF — the un-NAP'd row is what makes base = the roll's $1,843,549.)
6. **A credit union's / bank's "sales" in a 5-year sales report are deposit/transaction VOLUME, not retail sales** — exclude from `tenantSalesHistory` (e.g. Sun Federal CU "$1.66M" is deposits). Same caution for gyms (membership ≠ comparable retail PSF) — keep them but note non-comparability.
7. **Always run the four-way tie-out before delivering an owned deal:** (a) Σ occupied SF + Σ vacant SF = stated `totalSF`; (b) occupied/total = stated `occupancy`; (c) Σ occupied `annualRent` = `grossPotentialRent` = the roll's printed base total; (d) `effectiveGrossIncome` − `operatingExpenses` = `noi`. A node script over the JSON is the proof — never ship on eyeball.

## Analytical heuristics — apply in the grade/narrative (Eric's standing instruction, 6/22/26)
- **Demographics ↔ rent/cap (location quality):** higher household income AND higher population — ESPECIALLY when BOTH are high together — generally go with HIGHER rents and TIGHTER (lower) cap rates, and signal a dense **infill/urban** trade area where supply is constrained. Treat it as an interpretive lens, not a hard rule: a high-density, high-income location helps justify above-average in-place rent or a tighter going-in cap and supports mark-to-market upside; a thin or low-income trade area warrants more cap-rate cushion and weaker rent-growth assumptions. A deal whose rent/cap looks aggressive for a WEAK trade area is a pricing risk. Threaded end-to-end: the flat demographic fields (`population3mi`, `avgHHIncome3mi`/`medianHHIncome3mi`, `trafficCountVPD`) now flow into the roster-analysis snapshot (`buildRosterAnalysisSnapshot` in extract.ts) and the `LOCATION QUALITY` signal in `ROSTER_ANALYSIS_PROMPT` tells the grade to use them; the deal page already shows them. Keep extending this loop — when Eric teaches a market heuristic, fold it into the prompt AND the snapshot, not just memory.
- **Above-market rent is mark-to-market DOWNSIDE, never "upside" (Eric, 7/2/26).** An in-place rent well ABOVE the brand/library median is a premium that may not survive renewal — at the next expiry/option it can reset DOWN toward market, or the tenant leaves — and it INFLATES current NOI, so a buyer capping that NOI overpays for income that rolls off. NEVER call above-market rent "mark-to-market upside" or an automatic strength. It is SUSTAINABLE (defensible, mild strength) when the tenant's own sales/occupancy-cost support it — strong salesPSF and/or LOW occupancy cost. It is a RISK (rollover / NOI-quality) when sales are weak / occupancy cost is high, ESPECIALLY with a reset inside the ~5–7yr hold, sized to the tenant (a big anchor premium into a near-term reset is a headline risk). With no sales data (the common case), do NOT assume either way — flag it to VERIFY the sales support the premium. Threaded end-to-end: `deriveAboveMarketRolldownFlag` (aboveMarketRent.ts) is a token-free red flag gated so a low-occupancy-cost premium doesn't fire; `buildAugmentPrompt` rule 2 (tenantBenchmarks.ts) and `ROSTER_ANALYSIS_PROMPT` (extract.ts, "ABOVE-MARKET RENT" block) enforce the same. The one thing that HELPS a premium is a strong, rising trade area (rents climbing toward the in-place rent → lower rollover risk) — never scored as upside.
- **Movie theaters are judged on sales PER SCREEN, not PSF (Eric, 7/2/26).** A cinema is a big box, so its sales/SF is meaningless; theaters underwrite on sales per screen (and per seat). `theaterMetrics.ts` (`isCinema`, `resolveScreens`, `cinemaSalesRead`) captures the screen count — from the extracted `screens` field (Tenant), else parsed from the name (a trailing number on a cinema, "Regal … 14" → 14) — and derives sales/screen (salesPSF×SF ÷ screens), shown wherever theater sales render (roster, Tenant page) and fed to the grade (roster-analysis snapshot + a CINEMA signal in the prompt; ~$400–700k+/screen is healthy). **SAFEGUARD:** a real theater rarely exceeds ~30 screens, so a screen count >30 (or <3) is treated as SUSPECT — it never silently computes a per-screen figure, it shows a "⚠ confirm" prompt, the extraction prompt warns against it, and `audit-screens-implausible` (extractionAudit.ts) flags it for confirmation. Extend with per-seat if Eric asks.
- **Below-market rent locked by options is NOT a risk — it is secure, sticky income (Eric, 7/2/26).** A tenant never walks away from a below-market deal, so a locked below-market lease is the STICKIEST, most durable income in the center (near-zero vacancy/re-leasing risk). The only thing "lost" is theoretical upside that was never underwritten. So it is a NEUTRAL data point to call out (secure income; mark-to-market not capturable in the 5–7yr hold), NOT a red flag, and for a healthy credit it's a mild income-durability strength. NEVER frame a healthy locked tenant as "structural rent underperformance," "rent compression," or something that "persists through exit" as a negative. It becomes a genuine RISK only when: (i) the tenant is distressed (weak sales / high occupancy cost, so even the low rent is shaky), (ii) a DOMINANT ANCHOR's locked rent materially caps the asset's income growth and resale value (a valuation/exit point — the income is still secure), or (iii) the deal is priced as though that upside were capturable. Threaded end-to-end: `deriveOptionOverhangNote` (renewalOptionOverhang.ts) renders a NEUTRAL "Lease Economics" card on the deal page (not the red-flags box) with a `concern` line only for cases (i)/(ii); the MTM tool splits capturable vs 🔒-locked upside; and both the benchmark-augment prompt (`buildAugmentPrompt` rule 1b/1c in tenantBenchmarks.ts) and `ROSTER_ANALYSIS_PROMPT` (extract.ts) now forbid classifying locked below-market rent as a risk. **A single below-market inline tenant with cheap long options is never a "key risk."**

## Lease abstracting — document-completeness rules (HARD RULES; learned the hard way)
A missed King Soopers Third Amendment once produced a wrong abstract that flagged a CORRECT rent roll as erroneous. Never again. Before abstracting ANY lease, on this or any deal:
0. **NEVER ASSUME — flag uncertainty instead (Eric's standing instruction; we got burned on the McDonald's ROFR).** When a legal document is ambiguous, internally inconsistent, partly illegible/OCR'd, or the governing exhibit/amendment isn't in the folder, do NOT pick a value or state a clause as settled fact. Capture what the document appears to say, mark it UNVERIFIED (`verifiedAgainstExecutedDoc:false`), and raise a prominent flag telling Eric exactly what to confirm and that it may need counsel. This is most critical for deal-defining rights — **purchase options, ROFR/ROFO (especially anything affecting a pad sale), co-tenancy/kickout triggers, exclusives, termination rights, guaranty scope/caps.** A confidently-worded wrong abstract is far more dangerous than an honest "verify this." When in doubt, flag it and ask Eric or the lawyers — never paper over the doubt. **These uncertainty flags must stand on the DOCUMENTS ALONE — future deals have no KPR abstract to lean on, so a flag must never read "KPR/the abstract had this as X"; it states the document-level doubt by itself.** Comparing your read against an existing KPR abstract or the rent roll is a SEPARATE quality-check layer (it catches what their abstract missed) and belongs in a separate differences list — keep the two distinct.
1. **Enumerate the ENTIRE tenant Drive folder by `parentId` — never rely on a title/fulltext keyword search.** Files are often named by store number, "Fully Executed", or DocuSign export (e.g. `620-00072 - Third Amendment...`), with the tenant name nowhere in the title. List every file in the folder and read each one. A keyword search WILL silently miss the most important document.
1a. **A present-but-unreadable file is NOT a missing or "wrong" document — never mis-attribute it (the Nordstrom lesson).** If a lease is a large scanned image that won't OCR, say EXACTLY that ("large scanned file, couldn't read it"), mark the affected fields `verifiedAgainstExecutedDoc:false`, and flag it — do NOT invent an explanation (e.g., "this is the wrong center's/store's lease," "the base lease is missing") to cover for not reading it. Sanity-check identity before claiming a mismatch: a store/suite number that actually belongs to ANOTHER tenant in the deal is a sign YOU misread, not that the file is misfiled. And on the orchestrator side: **before relaying ANY "lease is missing / wrong document / wrong center" claim to Eric, independently re-enumerate that folder by `parentId` and look at the file yourself** — these claims must be verified, never passed through. (We told Eric a Nordstrom lease was missing when it was sitting in the folder; he caught it.)
1b. **START in the exact folder(s) Eric points you to, and STAY there — never go exploring into other folders on your own, and never pull in documents from a DIFFERENT property/deal's folder (Eric's standing instruction).** If the file/folder Eric specified is wrong, empty, or won't load, **STOP and come back to Eric** — flag it and ask ("the file you pointed me to won't open / looks wrong — is there another copy?") rather than wandering off to find a substitute yourself. The only time you explore beyond it is when you genuinely think Eric pointed you to the wrong spot, and even then you FLAG that belief to him first, you don't just do it. This refines rule 8: search hard for the operative version of a document *within this deal's scope*, but a similarly-named file in another property's folder is NOT a substitute — adopting it is an error. (We strayed into the Missouri "Plaza on the Boulevard"/Sansone OEA folder during Rockaway REA work and even reported it as Eric "mis-filing" Missouri docs into Rockaway — it was a separate deal we never should have touched; and we searched outside the pointed folder for the Town Plaza draft instead of asking. Both were the same mistake.)
2. **Walk the amendment chain to its end.** Amendments number sequentially (1st, 2nd, 3rd…). If you have a "Second Amendment," you must affirmatively prove there is no Third/Fourth before relying on it. Each amendment's RECITALS list the full prior chain — use them as a built-in completeness checklist. The controlling economic terms always come from the MOST RECENT amendment.
3. **Recency gate: compare your newest document's date to the rent roll's "as of" date.** A multi-year gap (e.g. newest doc 2021 vs roll "as of 2026") is a red flag that newer documents exist — go find them.
4. **The rent roll is management's live record. When it disagrees with your document set, the DEFAULT assumption is that YOU are missing a document — search for it first.** Only after an exhaustive folder sweep fails to find support may you flag a roll figure for Eric's review. Never declare the roll "wrong" to resolve a conflict you haven't run down. (KPR's own post-acquisition amendments are the usual source of "surprising" roll terms.)
5. When the roll shows terms you can't source (a later expiry, more options, a rent step), treat it as a treasure map to a missing amendment, not as an error to flag.
6. **Every document in the abstract's document list MUST be fully READ and APPLIED to the body (rent, options, dates, exclusives, flags).** It is a hard error to list a document (e.g. "Third Amendment — extends term to 2044, twelve new options") and then NOT carry its terms into the rent schedule/options, or — worse — to raise a flag claiming you "do not have" a document that is sitting in your own list. Before finalizing, reconcile the document list against the body: if a doc is listed, its terms are applied; if a flag says something is unconfirmed/missing, that doc must genuinely be absent from the list.
7. **The COMPLETE, OPERATIVE set of lease documents is the source of truth — the abstract must stand on its own.** The real cause of both past errors was working from an incomplete/superseded document set (missed King Soopers 3rd Amendment; abstracted the 1996 OfficeMax lease instead of the operative 2015 Office Depot A&R lease). The fix is document-discovery rigor, NOT deferring to the rent roll. The abstract must be correct and internally consistent from the documents alone — because sometimes there is **no rent roll at all, and the abstract is what we use to BUILD one.** Verify completeness from within the documents: the operative lease (most recent Lease / Amended-and-Restated Lease) + the full amendment chain, confirmed via each amendment's recitals (they list the full prior chain), with the most-recent amendment governing current term/rent.
8. **Find the OPERATIVE lease across the WHOLE Drive — don't anchor to the first folder.** A keyword search finds *a* folder, not necessarily the current one. Search the tenant across ALL of Drive (both brand names — "OfficeMax" AND "Office Depot", store-number variants, "Amended and Restated"). Folder/file names are signals: **"(Original)", "Old", "Superseded", "Prior" mean a NEWER lease exists elsewhere — go find it.** An **A&R Lease supersedes the original entirely** (relocations, downsizings and re-tenanting often come as a new/A&R lease, not an amendment to the old one).
11. **NO "not extracted this pass" PLACEHOLDERS — depth is non-negotiable.** Every abstract section must be genuinely abstracted from the documents, stated as a definitive "None" with a citation, or raised as a real flag if a governing document is truly missing. "Save bandwidth" governs WHICH tenants get abstracted (scope), NEVER how thorough each one is (depth). An abstract full of "per base lease (not extracted)" is worthless and must never ship. Before delivering, grep the file for "not extracted"/"not separately extracted"/"this pass" — zero hits.

10. **SURFACE EVERY MID-TERM TENANT LEVER AS A PROMINENT FLAG (Eric's standing instruction).** Any right a tenant can exercise mid-term to cut rent or get out is among the most important things an abstract conveys — elevate these to the flags section, don't bury them in the lease-notes. Capture and flag: **co-tenancy** outs (Alternative/Substitute/Reduced Rent + termination rights, with the trigger and named anchors), **sales/kickout** terminations (right to terminate if sales miss a threshold), **go-dark / cease-operations** terminations or recaptures, **exclusive-violation** rent abatement/termination, **early-termination options** (and mutual termination tails like Office Depot's), **ROFO/ROFR**, casualty/condemnation terminations, and any landlord obligation a tenant can offset against rent. For each: state the trigger, the remedy (rent reduction %/termination), the cure/notice windows, and whether it is currently in danger of being triggered (e.g. co-tenancy at its floor). When a lever is currently safe, still flag it as the thing to monitor.

   **CO-TENANCY TRIGGER FIDELITY — BE EXACT, NEVER OVER- OR UNDERSTATE (Eric's standing instruction, learned the hard way on Paddock Shops).** The single most important thing about a co-tenancy clause is WHAT actually trips it. Capture the trigger structure precisely and never collapse it:
   - **"ANY named tenant" clauses** ("Tenant gets relief if *any* Key Tenant fails to be open"): losing ONE named anchor DOES trip it — model each as a real single-anchor dependency.
   - **"X of N" clauses** ("at least 7 of these 10 Key Stores must stay open", "5 of 10 Named Tenants"): losing one (or even a few) named store does NOT trip it — you need (N−X+1) dark. NEVER model these as per-anchor solo triggers (that massively overstates exposure — e.g. implying "Barnes or Ulta alone loses a ton of rent" when the lease needs 4 of 10 dark). Record the EXACT named list, the X-of-N count, and the separate occupancy-% threshold (which is usually the real standalone trigger), with a verbatim quote. This must be bulletproof in the abstract.
   - **Occupancy-threshold clauses** ("below 75% of GLA occupied"): no named anchor at all — model as an occupancy trigger, don't invent anchor dependencies.
   - Always carry: the precise remedy (alternate/substitute rent = X% of gross sales, or 50% of minimum rent), the relief period before a termination right opens, and the notice window. When summary-sourced, mark `verifiedAgainstExecutedDoc:false` and say so — but unverified is NOT a license to be imprecise about the trigger.
   When a roll is available, reconcile SF, current rent, expiry and option count against it as a tripwire. A disagreement is a signal to INVESTIGATE, resolved in EITHER direction — it may mean (a) you are missing/using the wrong document (find the right one), or (b) the roll itself is wrong (MRI artifact, stale, data-entry error). Do not auto-defer to the roll and do not rationalize a mismatch away. Run it down in the documents; if the operative document set is complete and internally consistent and the roll still differs, FLAG the discrepancy for Eric (he may need to correct the roll) rather than bending the abstract to match. Per the cardinal flag rule: flag both for review, name the conflict, don't pick a silent winner.

## Abstract authority & self-audit (HARD RULES — Eric's standing instruction, learned 6/13/26)
The deliverable is an AUTHORITATIVE record, not a documents-only transcript. "I only wrote what the lease body stated" is NOT an excuse for an incomplete or non-actionable abstract.
1. **NEVER leave a KNOWABLE field null.** Executed docs govern the TERMS. But when a lease ties commencement / rent-start to "delivery" or "opening" and prints no calendar date, the real date for an **open** store is authoritative in the **rent roll / commencement-or-delivery memo / estoppel** — GO GET IT, fill the term dates AND date every rent step, and cite the source. Only leave a date null when the store genuinely has NOT commenced, and then say so explicitly (an explicit "not yet commenced — periods tie to the Commencement Date" flag). **A blank term date or an undated rent step on an OPEN tenant is a DEFECT, not "documents-only correctness."** (We shipped 10 of 36 Rockaway abstracts with null term dates / undated rent steps for tenants that were actually OPEN — ALDI was the only true not-yet-open one; Eric caught it.)
2. **SELF-AUDIT before delivering — surface YOUR OWN exceptions; never let Eric find them.** For EVERY tenant mechanically verify, and ship the audit alongside the abstracts: (a) term dates present OR explicitly "not commenced"; (b) every rent step dated; (c) `baseRentAnnual` reconciles to the current-period rent step; (d) the current-period rent reconciles to the roster within ~2% (a gap is a real signal — chase it, don't hide it); (e) SF reconciles to the roster; (f) options/co-tenancy/exclusive/guaranty populated or an explicit "None." Run it as a script over the whole set; list every exception in the cover notes.
3. **UNIFORM BAR across the whole deal.** Every tenant gets the same depth — no batch-to-batch drift. Before delivery, diff the set for outliers (some dated, some not; some with `baseRentAnnual`, some without) and fix them to the bar.
4. **Verify the ROUND-TRIP when abstracts feed app logic.** Confirm the app reads the data correctly — e.g., an undated rent schedule must not make the "roster vs lease" check grab a far-future option-period row and raise a false discrepancy. If the data shape can be misread, fix the data AND the reader.
5. **OWN scope/process errors; never blame the source's filing** or call your own gap a problem on Eric's end (reinforces rule 1b). If your abstract is missing something, say "my abstract is missing X," not "the document set is wrong."
6. **ABSTRACT FROM THE EXECUTED LEGAL DOCUMENTS ONLY — think like a lawyer (Eric's core standing instruction).** A lease abstract must be reproducible from the executed legal instruments ALONE, in this authority order: (1) the **lease + every amendment** (operative terms); (2) the **Commencement Date Agreement / Delivery Memorandum** — the both-party-signed document that memorializes the ACTUAL commencement when the lease ties it to "delivery/opening" and prints no calendar date (this, not the roll, is where a real commencement date comes from); (3) the **estoppel certificate** (tenant-signed certification of commencement/expiration/rent/deposit/no-defaults — strong corroboration). **The rent roll and KPR's draft abstracts are NOT sources — they are cross-checks/tripwires only**, the same tier as each other: management's unsworn record (MRI-generated, often stale/wrong — e.g. it lagged PetSmart's executed Third Amendment) and a teaching guide, respectively. When a roll or draft disagrees with the executed documents, the DOCUMENTS govern and I FLAG the roll/draft — I never pull a value FROM them into the abstract, and never bend the abstract to match them. If a commencement/delivery memo genuinely isn't in the document set, the honest output is "commencement not memorialized in the documents — flag," NOT a date silently backfilled from the roll or the draft. **LAST-RESORT fallback (Eric's clarification 6/13/26):** when the executed commencement/rent-commencement letter AND the estoppel are both absent from the documents, you MAY record the rent roll's date *provisionally* — but ONLY (a) marked `verifiedAgainstExecutedDoc:false` and (b) raised as a **missing-document gap + seller follow-up** ("request the executed rent-commencement letters"). Never silently, never as if document-sourced. (Even KPR's own lawyers' abstracts fell back to the rent roll where the commencement letters were missing — that is acceptable as a flagged last resort, not as the standard.) The whole point is to abstract a center where no rent roll and no draft exist. (6/13/26: I filled missing commencement dates first from KPR's draft workbooks, then "corrected" them from the rent roll — both wrong; the dates belong to the executed commencement/delivery memos + estoppels in the lease folders.)
